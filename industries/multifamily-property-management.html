Common-area electricity that quietly drains NOI.

Corridor and parking lighting, elevators, pool and amenity load, leasing-office HVAC — the owner-paid meters add up across a portfolio. We make 90+ suppliers compete for those accounts so the savings land where they belong: net operating income.

Why it matters

For multifamily, electricity is one of the few operating costs you can actually negotiate.

Taxes, insurance, and debt service are largely fixed. The common-area electric bill isn't — yet most properties pay whatever default-utility or auto-renewed rate they landed on years ago. In a deregulated market, the supply portion of that bill is open to competition, and a single contract decision can move the number for the life of the term.

Load profile
Steady, around-the-clock draw

Corridor and exterior lighting, parking garages, elevators, pumps, and security systems run 24/7. That flat, predictable load is exactly the shape suppliers bid most aggressively on.

Amenity centers
Pools, gyms & clubhouses

Pool pumps and heaters, fitness equipment, clubhouse HVAC, and EV charging stack onto the owner-paid meters — often the fastest-growing line on the community budget.

Portfolio scale
Many meters, many renewals

Across a portfolio, meters sit on different utilities and contract end dates. Miss a renewal window on one and it rolls to a punitive holdover rate. We track them so none get caught flat-footed.

How it works

You choose the supplier. We make them compete.

01
Utility still delivers

Your local utility keeps maintaining the wires and delivering power to every building. Nothing changes on-site for residents or staff, and reliability is unaffected — only who supplies the energy.

02
You choose the generator

In a deregulated state, the energy supply portion of your bill is open to competition. You pick which company generates and sells the electricity — and at what rate.

03
PDR makes suppliers compete

We submit your load profile to 90+ vetted suppliers simultaneously. They bid against each other in real time. The lowest bid wins your contract — and we charge no fees to your business.

Contract structures

The right structure depends on your portfolio's budget needs and risk tolerance.

Fixed rate
Locked price per kWh

One price per kWh for the full term, regardless of what markets do — so the common-area line in your operating budget stays predictable from January to December.

Best forOwners and managers who budget a year ahead and want zero surprises on the CAM line.
Index / passthrough
Market rate, no markup

Your rate floats with the monthly ISO settlement price. You take market risk but capture the downside when wholesale rates fall.

Best forPortfolios with the appetite to ride the market when conditions favor buyers.
Hybrid
Fixed base + float index

Lock a portion of your load at a fixed rate and leave the rest on index — balancing budget certainty against upside, and letting you layer in pricing over time.

Best forLarger portfolios that want certainty on most of the load with room to play the market.
