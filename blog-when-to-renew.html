When to renew your electricity contract (before the holdover rate hits)

June 18, 2026 · 5 min read

The single most expensive mistake in commercial energy is doing nothing. Here’s when to start your renewal — and how to avoid rolling onto a punishing month-to-month rate.

The holdover trap

When a fixed contract expires and you haven’t signed a new one, you don’t go back to a fair market rate — most suppliers roll you onto a month-to-month holdover (or “variable”) rate that can run far above market. It’s designed to be uncomfortable, and businesses can sit on it for months without noticing.

Bottom line: the worst rate you can have is the one you land on by doing nothing. Renewal is not a deadline to hit — it’s a window to work.

The ideal renewal window: 3–6 months out

Start shopping your renewal three to six months before expiration. Two reasons: it gives you time to run a real competitive bid instead of a rushed one, and it lets you watch the forward market and lock when pricing is favorable rather than on the day your contract happens to end.

Waiting until the last week forces you to accept whatever the market is that day — and removes your leverage, because the supplier knows you’re out of time.

Blend-and-extend: lower your rate early

If wholesale prices have fallen since you signed, you may not have to wait for expiration at all. A blend-and-extend blends your current rate with today’s lower market rate and extends the term — often dropping your price now while locking in future savings. It’s worth checking whenever the market has moved in your favor.

What to have ready

  • Your current contract’s expiration date and rate.
  • A recent utility bill (for usage and your ESI ID / account number).
  • Your annual kWh usage, if you have it.

That’s enough to run a full competitive bid. The best practice is to never let a contract reach its expiration date unattended — put the renewal window on the calendar the day you sign, or work with a broker who monitors it and re-bids every term for you.
