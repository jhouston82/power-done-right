How to compare business natural gas rates, on equal terms.

August 12, 2026 · 3 min read

Commercial gas offers rarely arrive in the same shape. One quotes per therm, another per dekatherm; one is fixed, another is index plus an adder; a third hides a usage limit in the fine print. Comparing them means putting every offer on the same footing first.

Supply vs. delivery: what is actually on the table

Your local gas utility owns the pipes in the street, delivers the gas, reads the meter and answers emergencies. Its delivery rates are regulated and stay the same no matter who supplies your gas.

In states with a choice program, the supply — the gas itself and getting it to the utility’s system — can be bought from a competitive supplier. That supply portion is the only part of the bill that competition can lower, so it is the only part your offers should be compared on. We currently serve commercial gas accounts in seven states, including Kentucky, Michigan and Virginia.

Get the units right

A therm is 100,000 Btu of heat. A dekatherm (Dth) is ten therms, or one million Btu (also written MMBtu). Many bills meter in volume — hundreds or thousands of cubic feet (Ccf or Mcf) — and convert to therms with a heat-content factor printed on the bill.

Convert every offer to the same unit before you compare. A price per dekatherm looks ten times larger than the same price per therm, and that mistake is more common than it should be.

Fixed, index, and in between

Fixed pricing sets one supply price per therm for the term. Index pricing moves monthly with a published benchmark — most often the NYMEX Henry Hub settlement — plus two further pieces: basis, the difference between the benchmark and the price at your regional delivery point, and the supplier’s adder.

Ask every index offer whether basis is included or passed through. A low adder with basis passed through is not comparable to an all-in fixed price. Our article on fixed vs. index pricing covers the risk trade-off; the same logic applies to gas.

The terms that hide in gas contracts

  • Swing or bandwidth. A fixed price usually covers a range around your expected usage. Usage outside that range may be priced at market or charged a penalty. Ask what the range is and what happens outside it.
  • Minimums. Some contracts bill a minimum volume whether or not you use it.
  • Pass-throughs. Pipeline, storage, balancing and utility fees may be included in the price or billed on top. Get the list in writing.
  • Start date and renewal. Check that the start aligns with a meter read, how early termination is calculated, and what rate applies if the contract rolls over.

Read the terms before the price. The lowest headline rate is often paired with the tightest swing limits.

A clean comparison in four steps

  • Pull twelve months of usage by month. Gas use is seasonal, and winter months usually drive the annual cost for businesses that heat with gas.
  • Normalize each offer to an all-in supply price per therm, with a written list of anything passed through.
  • Model the annual cost on your actual monthly profile rather than a flat average, so the heating months carry their real weight.
  • Compare terms — swing, minimums, termination and renewal — before you compare rates.

The simplest way to get comparable offers is to ask for them that way: the same usage profile, term options and contract requirements sent to every supplier at once. That is how we run a gas procurement, and you receive a written comparison of every offer with our recommendation.
