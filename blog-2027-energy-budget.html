Building your 2027 energy budget, line by line.

October 7, 2026 · 3 min read

For calendar-year companies, the fourth quarter is when next year’s energy line gets set — often by taking this year’s spend and adding a percentage. A more defensible budget separates what you have already locked, what still floats, and what operations can control.

Start with the contract calendar

List every electricity and gas account with its supplier, rate, structure and contract end date. Two groups need attention first: accounts on month-to-month or holdover pricing, which carry market risk today, and accounts whose contracts end during 2027, which carry it for every month after they expire. Our renewal guide explains how to find end dates if you do not have them on file.

Split the cost into three buckets

  • Locked. Supply under a fixed-price contract through its end date. Budget it at the contract rate times forecast usage; it is the most reliable line you have.
  • Floating. Index-priced supply, any months after a contract expires, charges your contract passes through at cost, and regulated delivery charges, which change when regulators approve new tariffs and riders.
  • Controllable. Usage and peak demand. These belong to operations rather than procurement, but they move both of the other buckets.

Forecast usage from your own data

Use twelve to twenty-four months of bills: monthly kWh or therms and, for demand-billed accounts, monthly peak kW. Then adjust for what you already know — a location opening or closing, longer hours, new equipment, EV chargers. If you need a starting point, our usage guide shows how to read the numbers.

Price the floating bucket as a range

A single number invites a surprise. Present a base case and a stress case for everything that floats, and say what drives the difference. For contracts expiring in 2027, ask for indicative pricing on the new term before the budget is final; forward prices move daily, so treat any quote as a snapshot rather than a commitment.

If the range is wider than the business can tolerate, that is a procurement decision, not just a budgeting one: locking part of the exposure now and leaving part open is a common middle path. Our article on fixed vs. index pricing covers the trade-offs.

A budget is only as firm as the contracts behind it. The locked bucket is the one you can defend line by line.

Line up procurement with the budget

A contract ending mid-2027 should not wait until mid-2027. Starting three to six months ahead gives suppliers time to compete and gives you a choice of when to lock; see when to renew. Put each procurement start date on the same calendar as the budget review, so the two decisions are made together.

A one-page energy budget memo

  • Every account, its contract end date and its pricing structure.
  • Forecast usage by month, with the assumptions behind it.
  • Locked cost, and the floating range with its drivers.
  • Operational actions on demand and usage, with an owner for each.
  • Procurement start dates for every contract ending in 2027.
